# Nature Mode

## Purpose

Use this mode for a Nature-style research article only after identifying the exact journal, article type, and submission stage. “Nature-style” is a narrative strategy; it is not a universal format shared by flagship *Nature*, *Nature Communications*, and every Nature Portfolio title.

## Reader and editorial test

For flagship *Nature*, write simultaneously for:

- a scientist outside the immediate specialty who must understand why the result matters;
- a domain expert who will inspect the evidence and mechanism;
- an editor deciding whether the advance changes a broad scientific conversation.

For another Nature Portfolio journal, derive audience breadth, significance threshold, and editorial test from that journal's current aims, scope, article type, and author instructions. Do not impose flagship-Nature breadth as a universal requirement.

The first page must answer, at the breadth appropriate to the exact journal:

1. What consequential question or phenomenon is at stake?
2. What exact knowledge gap prevents progress?
3. What did this work discover, demonstrate, or enable?
4. Which evidence makes that conclusion credible?
5. Why does the finding matter beyond the narrow system studied?

Avoid both extremes: generic public-facing hype and specialist detail before the reader has a conceptual map.

## Narrative spine

Use this default argumentative arc, adapting it to the actual article type:

1. **Importance:** establish the scientific problem and stakes.
2. **Known landscape:** state only the knowledge needed to expose the gap.
3. **Gap or tension:** specify what remains unresolved, unexplained, or inaccessible.
4. **Advance:** state the discovery, mechanism, dataset, method, or capability in direct terms.
5. **Evidence ladder:** order results so each resolves a necessary question and motivates the next.
6. **Broader meaning:** explain how the result changes understanding, capability, or future inquiry within supported scope.

Do not organize the Results section in experimental time order. A useful evidence ladder often moves through:

- existence or headline effect;
- specificity and controls;
- mechanism or explanatory evidence;
- boundary conditions, generality, or robustness;
- implication or consequence.

Use only the rungs the paper actually contains. Do not manufacture a mechanistic story from correlational evidence.

## Title and opening summary

Derive exact title and summary constraints from the current official instructions. Do not assume a generic “uncited abstract” convention: individual Nature-family journals and article types can differ, and official rules can change.

### Title

Prefer a concrete scientific advance or relationship over slogans. Avoid unexplained acronyms, unsupported universality, and titles that name only a tool while hiding the biological, physical, computational, or clinical question.

### Summary or abstract

Build a compact argument:

- scientific context and importance;
- precise unresolved gap;
- what was done or discovered;
- strongest supported result;
- broad implication with an explicit evidence boundary.

Use quantitative anchors when they carry meaning and are supported. Avoid a method inventory.

## Main-text architecture

Nature articles may not follow conventional IMRaD headings. Determine the exact journal's expected structure, then design the logical units below without forcing visible headings where they do not belong.

### Introduction/opening

The opening should:

- establish significance without a textbook preamble;
- identify the precise gap;
- state the response and main finding early;
- give the reader a map of the evidence;
- place a requested motivation or hero Figure 1 after the problem–gap–response has been stated.

Do not hard-code an introduction paragraph count. Paragraph boundaries should track conceptual moves.

### Results

Each subsection or logical block should have one scientific duty:

1. ask the question;
2. explain the evidence or experimental design needed to answer it;
3. report the observation;
4. interpret only what the observation warrants;
5. connect the conclusion to the next question.

Give negative controls, uncertainty, sample definitions, and alternate explanations the prominence required to interpret the claim. Do not bury an exception that changes the conclusion.

### Methods

Mirror the evidence architecture: readers should be able to trace each main result to the relevant method, analysis, data source, and statistical treatment. Exact placement, length, and online-only policies are venue- and stage-dependent and must be verified.

Separate:

- study or system design;
- sample/data acquisition and inclusion criteria;
- procedures and interventions;
- measurement and preprocessing;
- statistical or computational analysis;
- materials, code, data, and reporting information.

### Discussion or closing synthesis

Return to the scientific question, not the writing process. Distinguish:

- what the evidence establishes;
- the strongest supported interpretation;
- material limits and alternative explanations;
- why the finding matters more broadly.

Do not end with an apology cascade. Do not use the final paragraph to introduce unsupported applications.

## Figure 1 in Nature mode

When the user requests a motivation figure, make Figure 1 an editorial “why this paper” object. It may be:

- a conceptual problem–gap–advance diagram;
- a real phenomenon or representative observation paired with the proposed explanation;
- a restrained problem → intervention → consequence composite;
- a headline measured contrast if the data, not the mechanism, is the clearest motivation.

It is not automatically a full architecture diagram. It may include one compact result inset only if the evidence is supplied and the inset materially strengthens the motivation. Route by dominant content through the appropriate figure-design skill.

## Figure, table, and evidence sequence

Build the main visual sequence as an argument rather than an inventory. A possible sequence is:

- Figure 1: motivation, phenomenon, or hero claim;
- subsequent figures: evidence ladder in scientific dependency order;
- table: only where exact comparison or compact enumeration is more legible than graphics;
- Extended Data: robust secondary support, controls, validations, or detail allowed by the journal;
- Supplementary Information: permitted material that does not fit Extended Data or Methods.

This is a role sequence, not a fixed count. Verify the allowed number and type of display items. Keep evidence necessary for the central claim in the main article.

## Extended Data and Supplementary Information

Do not call these a generic “appendix” unless the journal does. Verify the permitted containers and their review/publication behavior.

Map candidate material as follows:

| Material | Default destination | Override condition |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence essential to headline claim | Main text | Never demote merely for space |
| Controls or robustness that validate but do not carry the main narrative | Extended Data, if allowed | Main text if they alter interpretation |
| Detailed protocols and analysis | Methods | Supplement only if official rules require it |
| Large auxiliary datasets, videos, or extensive supporting material | Supplementary Information, if allowed | Follow exact file/type limits |
| Reporting, data, code, ethics, and availability statements | Required official location | Never improvise placement |

Every numbered main-text, Extended Data, and supplementary non-table figure receives the same fact lock, three-option design pack, caption, accessibility, provenance, and final-size QA treatment.

## Nature prose pass

- Prefer active, concrete verbs and name the scientific actor.
- Replace field-internal shorthand with accessible terms at first use.
- Keep necessary technical precision; remove jargon that only signals membership.
- Open paragraphs with the scientific question or conclusion, not procedural throat-clearing.
- Use emphasis through order and evidence, not adjectives such as “remarkable” or “groundbreaking.”
- State broad relevance only after showing how the evidence connects to it.

## Nature-specific failure checks

- The first page delays the finding behind a literature survey.
- The paper assumes specialist knowledge before explaining importance.
- Results follow laboratory chronology rather than an evidence ladder.
- The headline claim depends on an item hidden in supplementary material.
- A correlation is written as mechanism or causation.
- The manuscript treats all Nature Portfolio journals as one format.
- The draft hard-codes title, summary, Methods, display-item, or reference limits from memory.
- Figure 1 is decorative, overly technical, or disconnected from the central discovery.
- Discussion repeats the Results instead of establishing broader meaning and limits.

Repair these before line editing.
