# Editorial desk

## Find the story

Use public conference records, papers, reviews, author replies, meta-reviews, decisions, official conference explanations, and voluntarily public author commentary. Prefer primary records. Treat social allegations as leads requiring verification.

For new candidates, search the requested topics and conferences using the current date. Distinguish an old case newly covered today from an event that happened today. A supplied case can proceed directly without inventing two alternatives.

Return a compact candidate table: case ID, paper, conference/year, research topic, dramatic tension, important counter-context, source access, and recommendation. Prefer complete stories over weakly sourced sensational claims. Do not promise a minimum number of suitable cases when the available evidence supports fewer.

Use these editorial shelves: scores versus outcomes; reviewer disagreements; rebuttal plot twists; claims versus experiments; conference process. Keep the existing research themes as the main topic taxonomy. A case may have several tags.

## Build the evidence record

For each source, record:

- Stable source ID, exact URL, source type, paper or discussion version, authoring date when known.
- Retrieval time when actually fetched, or `supplied` when only the user's provided extract was read.
- Access status: `primary_read`, `archive_read`, `supplied_extract`, or `blocked`.
- The claim supported, a short accurate excerpt or location, and relevant qualification.

For each factual claim, record the source IDs, whether it is a fact or an attributed interpretation, and whether it is verified directly, supported only by an archive, or unresolved. Do not claim to have read an original paper or review when only its title, abstract, search result, or secondary account was available.

Use current original records when accessible. If blocked, preserve the URL and already accessible evidence; continue supported work with an explicit limitation. Never turn a failed request into fabricated quotations or implied full verification.

Track review scores with their scale and stage. Preserve each available snapshot rather than overwriting history. A predicted score increase is not an observed increase. An acceptance decision is not proof of reviewer consensus. A score/outcome discrepancy alone is not evidence of corruption or a technical mistake.

For ICLR 2026 score stories, check and cite the official process retrospective: https://blog.iclr.cc/2026/03/31/a-retrospective-on-the-iclr-2026-review-process/ . It describes resetting ratings to pre-rebuttal and reassigning ACs. Attribute specific case details to the actual accessible source. Do not infer an individual acceptance cause from this conference-wide procedure.

## Write with a point of view

Build humor around observable contradictions, changing arguments, ambiguous standards, inflated claims, or awkward process details. Make the technical disagreement understandable in one or two sentences.

Separate four text modes: exact quotation, labeled translation, attributed paraphrase, and editorial joke. Do not put an invented line in a reviewer's mouth or present a generated image as an authentic review screenshot. Use short quotations within applicable source limits; paraphrase the surrounding argument.

Anonymous reviewers stay anonymous. Use public reviewer labels or edition-local R1/R2 labels with a documented mapping. Do not use leaked identities, guess motives, or invite readers to target a person.

Include the strongest relevant opposing explanation. If it changes what the headline implies, place it in the headline/subtitle pair or the first paragraph. The opening X post and cover must make sense when viewed alone.

## Complete the check

Check names, dates, decision, scale, arithmetic, versions, source scope, quotation accuracy, and source accessibility. Audit both languages independently against the same fact record. Keep satire distinct from claims about research integrity.

Return a short verdict: `ready`, `provisional`, or `hold`, with unresolved items. `ready` means the edition's stated evidence scope is adequately supported, not that the paper is scientifically correct. When a key premise depends on an inaccessible original, default to `provisional` and retain archive attribution. A provisional draft can be exported but must not silently become a fully verified story.
